Federated spatiotemporal AI · Catalonia

Catching broken air-quality sensors without sharing their data

Seven Barcelona monitoring stations each train a forecasting model on their own readings. Only model weights and a compressed summary of recent conditions ever leave a station. A server compares those summaries across stations to decide whether an unusual reading is real pollution or a failing instrument.

The decision, per reading

Every forecast is compared against what the sensor actually reported. When the gap is large enough to matter, the neighbouring stations arbitrate.

Trustworthy

The reading sits close to the forecast. Nothing to flag.

Real event

The reading is unusual, and nearby stations are seeing it too. A genuine pollution episode, not a fault.

Sensor fault

The reading is unusual and no neighbour agrees. The instrument is the odd one out.

Every model, side by side

The ladder runs from a trivial baseline to the proposed federated model. Rung 4 has no privacy constraint at all and exists to price the constraint: the gap between it and the federated rungs is what privacy costs.

Which faults get caught

Six fault types are injected at the rate and episode lengths measured in the real data. Only the corrupted value itself is synthetic. Stuck-at is the telling case: no model that looks only at a station's own history finds it, because a frozen sensor is easier to predict than a working one.

Two operating points

One threshold cannot serve both jobs. Spikes and dropouts are incidents worth an alert; drift and bias are calibration problems that belong on a maintenance list, where a cheap inspection beats months of quietly wrong data.

How much of this is certain

Paired block bootstrap, proposed model minus federated baseline at a 3-hour horizon. An interval that crosses zero means the data does not establish a difference. With seven stations, none of the aggregate differences do.

Data. European Environment Agency E1a verified air quality, Catalonia, 2017–2024. Meteorology from ERA5 reanalysis at each station's coordinates.
Why synthetic faults. Every reading the EEA flags as invalid has its value replaced by a no-data sentinel, so the real labels mark absent readings rather than wrong ones. Evaluation uses injected faults matched to the measured real rate of 2.805% and to real fault episode durations.
Caveat. Results come from one test window and seven stations. Differences between models are mostly smaller than their confidence intervals.
