Federated spatiotemporal AI · Catalonia

Catching broken air-quality sensors without sharing their data

Thirty Catalan monitoring stations each train a forecasting model on their own readings. Only model weights, a compressed summary of recent conditions, and a five-level indicator of the station's own drift ever leave a station. A server compares those summaries across stations to decide whether an unusual reading is real pollution or a failing instrument.

The decision, per reading

Every forecast is compared against what the sensor actually reported. When the gap is large enough to matter, the neighbouring stations arbitrate.

Trustworthy

The reading sits close to the forecast. Nothing to flag.

Real event

The reading is unusual, and nearby stations are seeing it too. A genuine pollution episode, not a fault.

Sensor fault

The reading is unusual and no neighbour agrees. The instrument is the odd one out.

Which faults get caught

Six fault types are injected at the rate and episode lengths measured in the real data. Only the corrupted value itself is synthetic. Spikes and noise are caught; a frozen, drifting or offset sensor is barely caught at all, because each is easier to forecast than a working one and so produces a smaller error, not a larger one. Widening the network from seven stations to thirty moves every one of those off zero, and nowhere near far enough.

Two operating points

One threshold cannot serve both jobs. Spikes and dropouts are incidents worth an alert; drift and bias are calibration problems that belong on a maintenance list, where a cheap inspection beats months of quietly wrong data.

Catching a drifting station

Slow calibration drift is invisible to a station's own forecast error -- a sensor that drifts slowly stays easy to predict, so the error shrinks rather than grows. Each station therefore uploads one five-level indicator of where it currently sits relative to its own history. A station whose level moves while its neighbours hold is drifting.

Does it transfer to Delhi?

The Catalonia-trained model applied unchanged to seven Delhi stations, with no retraining. Relative forecast error barely moves. What breaks is calibration: the residual threshold each station settles on varies far more widely there.

How much of this is certain

Paired block bootstrap at a 3-hour horizon, resampling both stations and time. An interval that crosses zero means the data does not establish a difference. At seven stations none of them did; at thirty, the recall gain and the precision cost both do.

Data. European Environment Agency E1a verified air quality, Catalonia, 2017–2024. Meteorology from ERA5 reanalysis at each station's coordinates.
Why synthetic faults. Every reading the EEA flags as invalid has its value replaced by a no-data sentinel, so the real labels mark absent readings rather than wrong ones. Evaluation uses injected faults matched to the measured real rate of 2.805% and to real fault episode durations.
Caveat. Results come from one test window. At thirty stations the recall gain and the precision cost both survive resampling; every other difference between models remains inside its confidence interval.
Compute. Trained on an NVIDIA RTX 4060 (CUDA 13.0). Earlier runs were accidentally CPU-only, which is why the node set took this long to widen.
